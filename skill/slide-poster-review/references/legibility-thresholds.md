# Text size by visual angle: evidence for the FS level boundaries

Compiled 2026-11-24 (QUEUE task 12) from a literature and standards review. Items marked
*unverified* were seen only in secondary sources.

**Conversions.** Cap height is taken as 0.7 em and x-height as 0.5 em. Cap arcmin = 3437.75 × cap / distance.
- Slides: cap′ ≈ pt × 4.456 / N, where N is the distance in multiples of image height and the slide is 540 pt high.
- Posters: cap′ ≈ pt × 849 / D(mm).

## Evidence

| Source | Measures | Threshold as stated | Cap-height arcmin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Snellen / ICO 1984 (logMAR 0) | Optotype (capital letter) height | 20/20 = 5′ | 5 (recognition limit) | 20/40 = 10′; 20/70 = 17.5′ |
| WHO ICD-11 | Presenting acuity | Mild impairment worse than 20/40; moderate worse than 20/60 | Threshold 10–18 | Many older viewers fall in 20/40–20/70 |
| Whittaker & Lovie-Kitchin 1993 | Print size ÷ acuity threshold | 2:1 fluent, 3:1 high-fluent | 20/20: 10 / 15. 20/40: 20 / 30 | "Acuity reserve" multiplier |
| Legge & Bigelow 2011 | x-height | Critical print size ≈ 0.2° (12′), range 0.14–0.24° | ≈ 17 (12–20) | Smallest size at maximum reading speed |
| ISO 9241-303:2011 | Capital E height | Minimum 16′; preferred 20–22′ | 16 / 20–22 | *unverified* (quoted via DIN excerpt / AOC report) |
| ANSI/HFES 100-2007 | Character height | Minimum 16′; should be 22–30′; ≥ 10′ for footnotes and subscripts | 10 / 16 / 22–30 | Uppercase basis inferred |
| MIL-STD-1472H | Character height | Labels: 15′ minimum, 20′ preferred. On-screen: 10′ minimum | 10–16 / 20 | 1472F: colored text 21′ minimum |
| FAA HF-STD-001B; NUREG-0700 r3 | Character height | 16′ minimum, 20–22′ preferred | 16 / 20–22 | Derived from MIL and ISO |
| AVIXA V202.01:2016 (DISCAS) | "Element" height | Farthest viewer = 200 × element height (17.25′) | 17 if cap height, ≈ 12 if em | Element not defined; 4H/6H/8H ≈ 2/3/4% element height |
| 2010 ADA 703.5.5 | Cap height (signs) | 5/8 in at under 6 ft, plus 1/8 in per foot | ≈ 30–36 | Low-vision signage; generous upper anchor |

| Poster guides (SPIE, UCLA, Erren & Bourne 2007) | Point size | Body ≥ 24 pt (SPIE 24–32) | 24 pt = 20′ at 1 m, 10′ at 2 m | Assume about 1 m |

## Summary

- Standards and reading research converge: **about 16′ cap height is the floor for legible reading**, and **about 20–22′ is preferred**. About 10′ is the absolute floor, suitable for footnotes only.
- Suggested boundaries: **10 / 16 / 22′**. Confidence: high for 16′, moderate–high for 10′ and 22′.
- At **8H** these give 18 / 29 / 40 pt on a 7.5 in slide, close to common rules of thumb. At 12H they give 27 / 43 / 59 pt.
- *Not used* (the project applies the same thresholds for everyone, as universal design). For older or low-vision audiences, scale by acuity (20/40) together with a 2–3× reserve. That gives **15 / 24 / 32′**. Confidence: moderate, since this is derived rather than taken from a standard.
- **Caveats:**
  - Projected slides have lower contrast than the displays these standards assume. Colored or low-contrast text may need about 1 level more.
  - Brief labels tolerate about 1 level less than continuous reading.
